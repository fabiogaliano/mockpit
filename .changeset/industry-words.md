Mockpit speaks the industry's words, with no aliases: a 3.x client (stdio MCP server, CLI, `mockpit watch`, the pi extension, the plugin) must be upgraded with the server. What you choose between is an **alternative**: `publish --alternative <name>`, `alternatives` in reads and the tree, question options bound with `alternative`, and the built-in question reads "Which alternative?" (MCP and HTTP the same; `--variant` on an idea now fails, naming `--alternative`). A **variant** is a form that ships: `publish --variants` or `edit <slug> --variants` says a screen's or component's entries all ship (a component's sizes, tones or feelings), so they are named with `--variant`, `read` lists them as `variants`, and nothing on it is chosen or nudged into an idea; `--no-variants` turns it off. Stored replies, drafts, questions, kept answers, forks and copies are rewritten in place on the first boot. What can go in a part is a **swap**: `data-swap` (with `data-swap-label`, `data-swap-default`), `publish --swaps part#swap=file` (MCP and HTTP `swaps`), an answer's `swap` beside its `set`, `suggestedSwapAsks`, and export's `allSwaps` and `all-swaps.html`; stored pages say `data-swap` on every part that had the old attribute. An included component is an **instance**: `<mockpit-instance slug="card">` (its wrapper says `data-mockpit-instance`), `instances` and `instanceIn` in reads and the tree, and the design screen's panel for them is titled Instances; stored pages, current and past, are rewritten to the new tag. A named combination of answers is a **combo** (`combos` on `read`, a reply's `combo`, Combos beside the design), and what the project's replies settled is its **decision log** (`decisionLog` on every read). A design opens by what it is, and nothing is remembered per design: a question the agent is waiting on opens Answers, an idea opens in Studio, a screen or component on Stage with **All states** as its second view, and a variants design as its grid. Each level has one status ladder, capitalised wherever it shows: a screen or component reads In design, Ready or Changed; an idea Exploring or In use in <target>; an alternative Kept, In use or Archived; a version current or Hidden. Taking an answer out of a question reads Archive, and Studio's bookmark reads **Keep**, with **Move** beside it.

Renamed with the words, each breaking for anything that used the old name:

- Routes: `/api/designs/:id/variants/archive`, `/restore`, `/:postId/poster` and `/:postId/restore` are now `/api/designs/:id/alternatives/…`.
- The renderer's `/s/:id?embed=` is now `?instance=`, and the viewer's `?variant=` link key is now `?alternative=`, so an old link loses the alternative it named.
- The push and webhook payload's `variant` is now `alternative`, and so is the event log's field.
- A design's notes are now its **spec**: `edit --notes` and `notes` are gone; write `edit --purpose` and `edit --behaviour` (MCP and HTTP `purpose`, `behaviour`). `edit --area-order` and `areaOrder` are gone too: `edit --areas` and `areas`, on every tier including the pi extension. Existing notes and area orders move over in place.
- `export` of a screen or component writes each entry as `<state>/<alternative>.html` with `<alternative>.history.json` (the start of the post id added where two names would share a file), plus `design.json` with its Ready mark, its spec and the components those versions are built from. Scripts that read a product design's `<state>/index.html` must read the entry's file instead; an idea still writes `index.html`.

A design carries its own record, so decisions stop living in side files: the spec reads What it is for, How it works and Decisions (the design's lines of the decision log). Agents write it with `edit --purpose` and `edit --behaviour "…" --state Now --part river`, and a line pinned to a part shows as a numbered pin on the stage while the Spec panel is open. A section you write by hand stands against agents until you clear it, and the agent hears your edit as `edited.spec`; a screen or component with no spec, or with decisions newer than it, is nudged on publish and in its feedback. Areas say what they are for (`edit --project <p> --area Food --description "…"`, or in place on the area page), and so does the project; `read --area Food` (MCP `read` with `area`, `GET /api/projects/:name/areas/:area`) gives an agent everything settled in one area, and Home's area page is the same view: description, screens, flows, components, decisions and the ideas waiting. Home's sidebar is now Product (Screens, Flows, Components, System), then the areas, then Ideas, and opens on the product.
