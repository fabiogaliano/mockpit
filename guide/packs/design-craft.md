## Rules

- Before designing something new, ask 2–3 short questions with likely answers
  as options (who, when, what matters most), then design. When the user asks for
  a quick draft, skip them and write your assumptions in each variant's note.
- Variants differ in one clear thing (layout, density, emphasis, voice), are
  named by what they are, and each note says when it's the best pick and what it
  costs. Three by default, five at most. A difference that is only colour or
  size is a knob, not a variant.
- Don't ask what has a right answer (big enough to tap, undo, readable in light
  and dark): decide it and say why in one line of the note. Ask only what is the
  user's to decide.
- Real content: realistic text and counts, and the awkward cases (a long name,
  a zero, many items). No lorem.
- No filler: no subtitles that say nothing, emoji, eyebrow labels, italic last
  words, pep talk, badges everywhere, decorative gradients or boxes in boxes,
  unless the user asks for that direction. Filler out, craft in: put the effort
  into hierarchy, real data and visuals that carry meaning. Never bare by default.
- Errors say what happened and what to do next. Nothing is cut off at the
  narrowest width.
- Once a direction is chosen, add the other situations the screen has (empty,
  many, error, loading) as situations of the state (`--situation`). Not in the
  first round.
- Once the user has chosen, the next version is that design with the likely fix
  applied, and the note says what changed. Don't reopen it with new options; ask
  one focused question only if it is still off.

## Suggestions

- One word per thing everywhere; buttons say what happens ("Log lunch", not "OK").
- Make things stand out on purpose: what looks different means something
  different, and the same meaning looks the same everywhere. Say the meaning in
  the note when it matters.
- Gestures the product is judged by are built working in Try mode, every phase
  including cancel. Not every button.
